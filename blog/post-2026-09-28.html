Why Your Home Office Has a Phantom Power Problem

Phantom power (also called standby power or vampire draw) is the electricity devices consume while switched off or idle. In a typical home office, devices in standby collectively draw 10–50 watts continuously. That sounds small, but run the numbers: 30 watts × 8,760 hours = 263 kWh per year. At the U.S. average rate of $0.17/kWh (EIA, 2025), that's roughly $45 per year — from a "turned off" desk.

"Standby power accounts for 5–10% of residential electricity use in the United States, costing the average household $100 or more per year."

— U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver Program

A smart power strip tackles this at the source. When it detects that your primary device (usually your PC or monitor) has powered down, it automatically cuts power to the peripheral outlets. No manual unplugging, no smart-home setup required for most models. That automatic behavior is what separates a genuinely money-saving device from a marketing gimmick.

How We Ranked These Strips

How to Get Maximum Savings From Any Smart Strip

The strip does the heavy lifting, but a few habits will maximize your results:

  • Audit your standby draw first. A $15 kill-a-watt meter lets you measure each device's idle draw before you buy. Focus the switched outlets on your biggest offenders.
  • Keep "always on" outlets for devices that need them. Your router, NAS, or a smart home hub should stay powered. Most multi-tier strips include one or two always-on slots for this purpose.
  • Don't rely on "sleep mode" alone. Sleep mode cuts display power but most PCs still deliver power to USB ports, keeping phones, webcams, and headsets charging indefinitely.
  • Combine with a smart thermostat. If you're cutting phantom load at the desk, consider tackling HVAC simultaneously. The combined savings can push $200–$300/year — see our smart thermostat guide for specifics.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can a smart power strip save on my electricity bill?

According to the U.S. Department of Energy, idle electronics in standby mode can account for 5–10% of a home's electricity use. A smart power strip that eliminates phantom load in a home office can realistically save $50–$100 per year depending on your equipment and local utility rates.

What is the difference between a smart power strip and a regular power strip?

A regular power strip simply distributes power to multiple outlets with no intelligence. A smart power strip detects when a primary device (like your PC) is off and automatically cuts power to peripheral outlets, eliminating standby or phantom energy drain from devices like monitors, speakers, and printers.
